LESSON 01

What is Prompt Engineering?

Prompt engineering is the practice of designing, structuring and refining instructions given to an AI system to guide its output toward a desired result.

Prompt Engineering అంటే AIకి ఇచ్చే instructions‌ను జాగ్రత్తగా రూపొందించడం, అవసరమైన వివరాలు జోడించడం, వచ్చిన result ఆధారంగా prompt‌ను మెరుగుపరచడం.

Why Does Prompt Quality Matter?

  • It communicates your creative intention.
  • It helps describe the subject and environment.
  • It guides the visual style and composition.
  • It can reduce unwanted variations.
  • It makes your workflow more repeatable.

Basic Prompt

A dog.

Improved Prompt

A golden retriever running across a green meadow, joyful expression, soft morning sunlight, cinematic photography, natural colors, medium-wide composition.

రెండో prompt‌లో subject, action, location, lighting మరియు visual style స్పష్టంగా ఉన్నాయి.

Prompt Engineering Is Not Magic

A longer prompt is not automatically a better prompt. Clear, relevant and consistent instructions are more useful than a collection of unrelated descriptive words.

ప్రతి model prompt‌ను వేర్వేరు విధంగా interpret చేయవచ్చు. అందువల్ల ఒకే prompt అన్ని models‌లో ఒకే result ఇస్తుందని అనుకోకూడదు.

LESSON 02

The Complete Prompt Structure

A structured prompt helps organize the information required for a creative generation task.

ఒక professional prompt‌లో ఏమేమి ఉండాలో తెలుసుకోవడానికి ఈ structure ఉపయోగించవచ్చు. ప్రతి prompt‌లో అన్ని sections తప్పనిసరి కావు. Project అవసరాన్ని బట్టి వాటిని select చేసుకోవాలి.

10-Part Prompt Framework

  1. Subject: Main person, object or scene.
  2. Action: What is happening?
  3. Environment: Where does it happen?
  4. Appearance: Important visual details.
  5. Composition: How is the scene framed?
  6. Camera: Viewpoint and camera behavior.
  7. Lighting: Light source and mood.
  8. Style: Visual or artistic direction.
  9. Motion: Movement, when creating video.
  10. Constraints: Details to avoid or preserve.

Example: Product Advertisement

Subject: A premium black wristwatch.

Action: The watch is displayed on a polished stone platform.

Environment: A dark luxury studio.

Appearance: Detailed metallic body, clean glass surface and elegant design.

Composition: Close-up product shot, centered composition.

Camera: Three-quarter angle.

Lighting: Controlled studio lighting with soft highlights.

Style: Premium commercial photography.

Constraints: No extra watches, no distorted product geometry.

Why Use This Structure?

If the result is wrong, you can identify which part of the prompt needs improvement instead of rewriting everything randomly.

LESSON 03

Subject, Action & Environment

The subject is the main focus of the generation. Action describes what the subject is doing. Environment describes the surrounding location.

Subject అంటే ప్రధానంగా కనిపించాల్సిన వ్యక్తి లేదా వస్తువు. Action అంటే అది ఏం చేస్తోంది? Environment అంటే అది ఎక్కడ ఉంది?

1. Subject

Examples:

  • A young astronaut.
  • A vintage motorcycle.
  • A futuristic robot.
  • A luxury perfume bottle.
  • A traditional Indian dancer.

2. Action

Examples:

  • Walking slowly.
  • Running toward the camera.
  • Looking toward the horizon.
  • Holding a product.
  • Turning around naturally.

3. Environment

Examples:

  • A busy Indian street.
  • A futuristic city.
  • A quiet mountain village.
  • A luxury studio.
  • A tropical beach at sunrise.
Combined Prompt:
A young astronaut walking slowly across a red desert landscape, distant futuristic space station, dramatic sunset in the background.

Common Mistake

ఒకే subject‌కు ఒకేసారి చాలా conflicting actions ఇవ్వకండి. ఉదాహరణకు, ఒక character simultaneously standing still, running and sitting అని రాస్తే model‌కు contradictory instructions అందుతాయి.

LESSON 04

Camera Angles, Shots & Composition

Camera language describes how the viewer sees the subject. It helps communicate framing, perspective and visual emphasis.

Camera angle, shot size మరియు composition ద్వారా మనం subject‌ను ఎలా చూపించాలనుకుంటున్నామో AIకి వివరించవచ్చు.

Common Shot Types

Shot Meaning Typical Use
Extreme Wide Shot Very wide view of the environment. Landscape and scale.
Wide Shot Subject shown with surrounding space. Establishing a scene.
Full Shot Entire person or object in frame. Character presentation.
Medium Shot Usually waist-up framing. Dialogue and action.
Close-Up Face or important object fills the frame. Emotion and detail.
Extreme Close-Up Very tight detail. Eyes, texture and product details.
Over-the-Shoulder Camera looks past one subject. Conversation and interaction.

Camera Angles

Angle Visual Effect
Eye-Level Natural and direct perspective.
Low Angle Subject may appear powerful or imposing.
High Angle Subject is viewed from above.
Bird's-Eye View Top-down view of a scene.
Worm's-Eye View Very low viewpoint looking upward.
Dutch Angle Tilted frame creating visual tension.

Composition Techniques

  • Rule of Thirds: Position important elements along a 3×3 grid.
  • Centered Composition: Place the main subject in the center.
  • Symmetry: Use balanced visual arrangement.
  • Leading Lines: Guide the viewer's attention.
  • Negative Space: Leave intentional empty space.
  • Foreground: Include elements close to the camera.
  • Background: Describe what appears behind the subject.
  • Depth: Separate foreground, subject and background.
Cinematic wide shot, low-angle camera perspective, strong foreground composition, distant mountains, subject positioned on the right third of the frame, atmospheric depth.
LESSON 05

Lighting, Color & Visual Style

Lighting and color strongly influence the mood, atmosphere and appearance of an AI-generated image.

ఒక scene ఎలా కనిపించాలి, దాని mood ఎలా ఉండాలి అనే విషయాలను Lighting మరియు Color వివరించడంలో సహాయపడతాయి.

Lighting Terminology

Term Meaning
Soft Lighting Gentle light with softer shadows.
Hard Lighting Strong light with defined shadows.
Backlighting Light coming from behind the subject.
Rim Lighting Light outlining the subject's edges.
Golden Hour Warm sunlight near sunrise or sunset.
Blue Hour Cool ambient light around twilight.
Volumetric Lighting Visible light beams interacting with atmosphere.
Studio Lighting Controlled lighting setup for a subject.
Silhouette Dark subject against a brighter background.

Color Terminology

  • Warm Palette: Reds, oranges and yellows.
  • Cool Palette: Blues, greens and blue-violets.
  • Monochromatic: Variations of one color.
  • Complementary Colors: Contrasting colors.
  • Muted Colors: Less saturated colors.
  • High Contrast: Strong differences between light and dark.

Visual Style Terminology

  • Photorealistic.
  • Cinematic realism.
  • Documentary photography.
  • Editorial fashion photography.
  • Anime-inspired illustration.
  • Stylized 3D animation.
  • Minimalist product design.
  • Surreal conceptual art.
Lighting Practice Prompt
A lone traveler standing on a mountain peak, warm golden-hour backlighting, soft atmospheric haze, long natural shadows, cinematic landscape photography, muted earthy color palette.
LESSON 06

Lens, Focus & Camera Language

Camera and lens terminology can help describe perspective, framing and depth of field.

Lens focal length, focus మరియు depth of field గురించి తెలిసి ఉంటే cinematic prompts‌ను మరింత స్పష్టంగా రాయగలుగుతారు.

Focal Length Basics

Focal Length Typical Perspective
14mm–24mm Very wide field of view.
24mm–35mm Wide environmental perspective.
50mm Natural-looking perspective.
85mm Portrait-oriented framing.
135mm and above Tighter framing and compressed perspective.

These are common full-frame photography references. Actual field of view depends on sensor size and camera system. AI models may interpret lens descriptions approximately.

Focus Terminology

  • Shallow Depth of Field: Subject in focus with a blurred background.
  • Deep Depth of Field: More of the scene appears in focus.
  • Bokeh: Visual quality of out-of-focus highlights.
  • Rack Focus: Focus shifts from one subject to another.
  • Sharp Focus: Subject details appear clearly defined.
  • Soft Focus: A deliberately softer visual appearance.

Camera Movement Terminology

Movement Meaning
Pan Camera rotates horizontally.
Tilt Camera rotates vertically.
Dolly In Camera moves closer to the subject.
Dolly Out Camera moves away from the subject.
Tracking Shot Camera moves along with the subject.
Crane Shot Camera moves vertically or through space using a crane-like movement.
Orbit Camera moves around the subject.
Zoom Focal length changes to alter framing.
A dolly movement and a zoom are not the same. A dolly physically changes the camera position; a zoom changes the lens focal length.
LESSON 07

Advanced Image Prompt Engineering

Image prompts should describe the visual result: subject, details, composition, lighting, style and the elements that must remain consistent.

Image generationలో scene ఎలా కనిపించాలో ముఖ్యమైన వివరాలు prompt‌లో ఉండాలి. Subject, facial appearance, clothing, background, camera view మరియు lighting వంటి వివరాలను అవసరానికి అనుగుణంగా ఇవ్వాలి.

Image Prompt Formula

Subject + Appearance + Action/Pose + Environment + Composition + Lighting + Style + Quality Details + Constraints

Example 01 — Cinematic Portrait

A cinematic portrait of a young Indian man standing on a rooftop in an urban city, wearing a dark navy jacket, natural facial proportions, thoughtful expression, distant city skyline, warm sunset backlight, medium close-up, 85mm portrait photography, shallow depth of field, realistic skin texture, professional cinematic color grading.

Example 02 — Product Photography

A premium wireless headphone displayed on a clean black pedestal, elegant industrial design, subtle metallic reflections, dark luxury studio environment, controlled softbox lighting, three-quarter product view, precise product geometry, premium commercial photography.

Example 03 — Indian Festival Scene

A vibrant Indian festival street at night, beautifully illuminated traditional decorations, colorful lights, families celebrating in the distance, realistic architectural details, warm ambient lighting, cinematic wide shot, rich but balanced color palette, documentary photography style.

Example 04 — Fantasy Environment

An ancient floating temple above a sea of clouds, enormous stone pillars, waterfalls flowing into mist, distant mountains, dramatic sunrise, atmospheric depth, epic fantasy environment concept art, wide-angle composition, intricate architectural details.

Example 05 — Fashion Editorial

A high-fashion editorial portrait of a model wearing a modern black outfit, standing in a minimalist architectural studio, dramatic directional lighting, clean composition, elegant pose, sophisticated monochromatic color palette, professional fashion photography.
These prompts are learning examples, not guaranteed output recipes. Different image models interpret descriptions differently. Check the selected model's own prompt guidance.
LESSON 08

Advanced Video Prompt Engineering

Video prompts must describe not only what is visible, but also how the scene changes over time.

Image prompt‌లో ఒక moment‌ను describe చేస్తాం. Video prompt‌లో ఆ scene ఎలా ప్రారంభమవుతుంది, subject ఎలా కదులుతుంది, camera ఎలా move అవుతుంది, చివరికి ఏమి జరుగుతుంది అనే విషయాలు కూడా చెప్పాలి.

Video Prompt Formula

Subject + Starting State + Action + Environment + Camera Movement + Motion Details + Lighting + Ending State + Visual Style

Example 01 — Character Walking

A young traveler walks slowly along a quiet mountain road at sunrise. The camera tracks backward smoothly while maintaining a medium shot. Natural walking motion, gentle wind moving the jacket, distant mountains covered in morning mist, soft golden sunlight, cinematic realism.

Example 02 — Product Reveal

A premium perfume bottle stands on a reflective black surface. The camera slowly pushes in toward the bottle as soft highlights travel across its glass edges. Fine mist moves gently in the background. Controlled luxury studio lighting, elegant commercial product film.

Example 03 — Cinematic Landscape

Aerial camera slowly flies forward over a vast green valley surrounded by towering mountains. Clouds drift naturally across the peaks. A river reflects the morning light below. Smooth cinematic drone movement, atmospheric depth, realistic landscape cinematography.

Example 04 — Character Turns

A woman stands near a large window, initially looking outside. She slowly turns her head toward the camera, maintaining a natural facial expression. The camera remains steady in a medium close-up. Soft daylight, realistic movement, cinematic portrait style.

Video Prompt Best Practices

  • Describe one clear primary action.
  • Specify the starting state.
  • Describe the camera movement.
  • Keep movement physically plausible.
  • Specify important environmental motion.
  • Describe the ending state when relevant.
  • Avoid conflicting instructions.
  • Use short, clear action sequences for short clips.
Some video models interpret camera directions differently. Some tools provide separate camera controls. When a dedicated control exists, use it rather than relying only on prompt text.
LESSON 09

Negative Prompts & Prompt Constraints

A negative prompt or constraint describes unwanted elements that the system should avoid, where supported.

మనకు ఏవి కావాలో చెప్పడం మాత్రమే కాకుండా, ఏవి వద్దో కూడా కొన్ని tools‌లో specify చేయవచ్చు. దీన్నే Negative Prompting అంటారు.

Common Negative Prompt Examples

blurry, low detail, distorted proportions, duplicate objects, unwanted text, watermark, unnatural anatomy

Where Negative Prompts Can Help

  • Reduce unwanted visual elements.
  • Discourage duplicate objects.
  • Specify unwanted composition outcomes.
  • Reduce certain image artifacts.
  • Help maintain a cleaner visual direction.

Important Limitations

Not every model provides a separate negative prompt field. Some models ignore or interpret negative instructions differently.

A negative prompt cannot guarantee that all errors will disappear.

Constraints in Video

In video generation, constraints may describe how the subject, background or camera should behave.

The camera remains fixed. The subject stays in the center of the frame. No scene cuts. Keep the background architecture consistent. Natural movement only.
Do not add a long list of negative terms without a reason. Focus on the specific failure you are trying to prevent.
LESSON 10

Prompt Iteration, Debugging & Consistency

Prompt iteration is the process of testing a prompt, reviewing the result and making controlled improvements.

మొదటి prompt‌లోనే perfect result రావాల్సిన అవసరం లేదు. వచ్చిన output‌ను పరిశీలించి, ఏ అంశం తప్పుగా ఉందో గుర్తించి, ఆ భాగాన్ని మాత్రమే improve చేయాలి.

Five-Step Improvement Process

  1. Generate: Create an initial result.
  2. Review: Identify the main problem.
  3. Diagnose: Find which instruction was unclear.
  4. Refine: Change only the relevant prompt section.
  5. Compare: Review the new result against the previous one.

Example: Prompt Debugging

Initial Prompt

A man in a city, cinematic.

Problem

The output shows an unclear location, an unexpected camera angle and an inconsistent visual style.

Improved Prompt

A young man standing on a rooftop in a modern Indian city at sunset, medium-wide eye-level shot, subject positioned on the left third of the frame, warm backlighting, realistic urban architecture, cinematic photography, natural proportions.

Improved prompt‌లో subject, location, framing, lighting మరియు style స్పష్టంగా ఉన్నాయి.

Character Consistency

ఒకే character‌ను multiple scenes‌లో చూపించాలంటే appearance details‌ను consistent‌గా maintain చేయాలి.

Describe relevant details such as:

  • Approximate age appearance.
  • Hair style and color.
  • Clothing and accessories.
  • Distinctive visual features.
  • Body proportions and general appearance.

Where supported, use reference images or dedicated character identity features. Text alone cannot guarantee exact identity consistency across every model.

Reference Image Prompting

A reference image can guide appearance, composition, color, environment or other supported visual attributes.

Always check what the selected model actually uses the reference for. Some tools preserve subject identity; others mainly use style, composition or visual guidance.

Common Prompt Mistakes

Mistake Improvement
Too vague Add relevant subject and scene details.
Conflicting instructions Choose one clear visual direction.
Too many actions Describe one primary action.
Unclear camera direction Specify shot type and movement.
Unnecessary keywords Remove irrelevant descriptions.
Ignoring model capabilities Check model-specific guidance.
Changing everything at once Improve one prompt section at a time.

KNOWLEDGE CHECK

Module 04 — Final Quiz

1. What is Prompt Engineering?

Designing and refining instructions to guide an AI system toward a desired output.

2. What are the main parts of a structured prompt?

Subject, action, environment, appearance, composition, camera, lighting, style, motion and constraints.

3. What is a Subject?

The main person, object or scene in a prompt.

4. What is a Close-Up shot?

A tight framing that emphasizes a face or important object.

5. What is Backlighting?

Lighting that comes from behind the subject.

6. What is Depth of Field?

The range of distances that appear acceptably sharp in an image.

7. What is the difference between Pan and Tilt?

Pan is horizontal camera rotation; tilt is vertical camera rotation.

8. What is a Negative Prompt?

An instruction describing unwanted elements, where the selected model supports it.

9. What is Prompt Iteration?

Testing, reviewing and refining prompts in a controlled process.

10. Does a long prompt always produce a better result?

No. Clear, relevant and consistent instructions are more important than unnecessary length.

11. What is a Reference Image?

An image supplied to guide supported visual attributes in a generation workflow.

12. Why should you check model-specific prompt guidance?

Different models interpret prompts and supported controls differently.
